# audience.md — Cheikh Sambakhe

## Who They Are

Coaches and consultants with mild, real success — not beginners. Making roughly **$10K/month**, so the business works, but lead generation is inconsistent. They're not asking "how do I get my first client," they're asking "how do I stop this from being random."

## The Problem I Solve

Inconsistent lead generation. They have a business that runs, but the pipeline isn't predictable — some months are full, some are dry, and there's no repeatable system behind it.

## What I Sell

Lead generation systems: funnels, ads, and course creation (via the Course Creation Framework) — built to turn the coaching/consulting business into something with a repeatable client pipeline, and to package their expertise into a sellable course as an additional lead-gen and revenue layer.

**Pricing:** case-by-case, not fixed yet.

## Real Language Found

### Pain Points

- The core cycle: business is either overloaded or dead quiet, with <cite index="15-1">no steady state in between — either working day and night to keep up, or wondering how much is left in savings and whether the phone will ring again</cite>.
- Content is not the bottleneck, conversion is: coaches post constantly, but as one creator put it, Instagram <cite index="38-1">feels good for visibility, but not for business</cite>.
- Agencies and consultants describe being great at delivery but weak at pipeline — <cite index="36-1">struggling with consistent leads</cite> even after years in business.
- On the course side: creators report doing everything "right" — building the product, launching — and still <cite index="51-1">nobody bought</cite>.
- No-shows and flaky discovery calls are a recurring frustration, though <cite index="41-1">most no-shows are preventable</cite> with the right system in place before the call.
- The overwhelm isn't from one bad tactic, it's from trying everything at once: chasing new offers, testing "secret" tactics from Twitter, posting everywhere — and still <cite index="53-1">not knowing where the next sale is coming from while burning through cash</cite>.

### Fears

- Fear of having built something real (certifications, following, a "professional-looking" brand) and still having nothing to show for it: <cite index="44-1">"I had everything… except results."</cite>
- Fear that the money running out is a personal failure, not a market problem — tied directly to the feast/famine cycle above.
- Fear of being one of the "97%" who build something (a course, a funnel) that never actually launches or sells.

### Dreams / Desired Outcomes

- A pipeline that runs without them chasing: language like <cite index="13-1">"tired of chasing leads that go nowhere"</cite> shows the dream is inversion — leads coming to them, not the other way around.
- Concrete, fast proof of a working system, not more theory — e.g. one coach reporting <cite index="5-1">35 appointments in 4 weeks, finally consistent client flow</cite> after implementing a real system.
- Freedom to choose clients instead of taking anyone who shows up — the "chasing → choosing" shift shows up repeatedly across testimonials and posts.

## How to Use This

- Lead with the **feast/famine framing** — it's the most emotionally accurate description of the $10K/month coach's actual problem, more precise than "inconsistent leads."
- The dream isn't "more leads" in the abstract — it's the **chasing → choosing** flip. Use that contrast directly in hooks and CTAs.
- On the course side, the wound is "I built it and nobody came" — not "I don't know how to build a course." Speak to distribution and demand, not production.
- Avoid generic "get more clients" language — this audience has heard it a thousand times and it reads as noise. Specificity (a number, a real scenario, a named moment like a ghosted discovery call) will cut through where abstraction won't.
